Research Experience CV

Cleiton Neves Santos

I study how teams of robots decide, in real time, who intercepts what.

I'm a PhD candidate in Computer Science at the Federal University of Minas Gerais (UFMG), Brazil, working at VeRLab with Prof. Douglas G. Macharet. My thesis treats multi-robot perimeter defense as a dynamic vehicle routing problem, pairing complexity results with algorithms that come with performance guarantees.

I came to robotics from mathematics and from industry software work, so I care about whether an idea holds up both on paper and in running code.

Research and publications

Intruders arrive over time at the boundary of a region, and a team of robots has to decide which defender goes after which intruder, in what order, and when to work alone or join forces.

Team formation and coalitions

Some intruders are more likely to evade capture. I study when defenders should form coalitions and how to keep those coalitions flexible as new threats appear, using probabilistic capture models.

  • Adaptive Team Formation for Collaborative Perimeter Defense M.Sc. thesis, UFMG, 2025. 3rd place in the Thesis and Dissertation Contest of the Brazilian Robotics Symposium (SBR 2025); finalist for the Master's Thesis Award at the Brazilian Symposium on Operations Research (SBPO 2026). Heterogeneous teams

Teams mix robots with different speeds and capabilities. I give an exact formulation of the allocation problem as an unsplittable flow, prove it is NP-complete and W[1]-hard, and propose a heuristic that stays within 0.3% of the optimum.

  • Manuscript under review C. N. Santos and D. G. Macharet. Available on request

Multiple regions of interest

Often there is not one perimeter to protect but several, spread across the environment. Defenders then have to decide how to split the team across regions as intruders arrive at each of them.

  • Multi-agent Perimeter Defense of Spatially Distributed Regions L. R. D. Paes, C. N. Santos and D. G. Macharet. Brazilian Conference on Robotics (CROS), 2026.

Moving perimeters

When the protected region itself moves, assignments that were feasible a moment ago can stop being so. I am developing a two-layer policy that keeps feasibility guarantees in this setting, evaluated under simulated actuator limits.

  • Manuscript in preparation C. N. Santos and D. G. Macharet.
